## 1. Executive finding
The strongest current *source-family hypothesis* is **productized B2B software (vertical SaaS / marketplace-distributed apps / APIs and AI-enabled software)**. This is a provisional FIRST research and qualification lane, not yet a final product selection or revenue forecast.

Why it leads the initial screen:
- High-value business buyers can support recurring subscriptions and higher annual contract values than low-priced consumer downloads.
- Marketplace ecosystems provide measurable paid software demand and existing distribution.
- The source can compound through subscriptions, seat expansion, usage billing, add-on modules, licensing and multiple products.
- A software product can be built once and delivered repeatedly, aligning with self-serve and automation constraints.

However, no source is declared proven for this owner until a concrete buyer segment, differentiated product, reachable distribution path, seller eligibility, India payout and positive fully-loaded unit economics are verified. RBI's broad software-services export figures are macro evidence of an existing market, not SaaS TAM and not directly obtainable revenue.

## 2. Important modelling correction: revenue engine vs distribution rail
Do not double-count a product as several revenue sources because it is sold on several platforms.

- **Revenue engine / monetization model:** subscription SaaS, usage/API billing, per-seat licensing, one-time digital sale, royalty, sponsorship, affiliate commission, advertising.
- **Distribution / payout rail:** Atlassian Marketplace, Shopify App Store, Microsoft Marketplace, Apify Store, Getvik, KDP, Envato, GitHub Sponsors, direct self-serve website or another eligible marketplace.
- **Product/IP asset:** the software, data product, template, book, game, media asset or agent that is sold/licensed.

A single B2B SaaS product listed on Atlassian and Microsoft is one product engine with two distribution rails. Revenue may be recorded by transaction and source, but the same customer payment must only contribute once to portfolio PCNE.

## 3. Evidence batch

### 3.1 India software export ecosystem — macro capacity, not direct product demand
RBI's 18-Sep-2026 annual survey reports 2025-26 software-services exports of **US$221.4B**, up 8.2% year-on-year; including foreign-affiliate sales, total software export services were **US$239.3B**. 91.7% of software-services exports were delivered off-site; the US accounted for 54.1% and Europe 31.8% of export destinations.

Critical disaggregation: within the US$221.4B figure, RBI reports IT Services at **US$147.0B**, BPO at **US$56.0B**, Engineering Services at **US$12.0B**, and Software Product Development at **US$6.4B** (2.9% of the total). The mission excludes manual services/recurring human fulfilment. Therefore, the full US$221.4B cannot be treated as the addressable self-serve SaaS market. The product-development component is the closer, though still imperfect, indicator.

Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63625

### 3.2 Atlassian Marketplace — verified paid software ecosystem
Atlassian reported on 26-Oct-2026 that its Marketplace exceeded **US$7B lifetime paid sales**; the latest US$1B was transacted in nine months. As of end-June 2026, more than 40% of Atlassian customers had at least one Marketplace app installed; the ecosystem included 6,000+ Cloud apps, 3,700+ public Forge apps and 10,000+ custom Forge apps.

This validates substantial buyer spend and distribution, not the probability that a new app will succeed. Competition and exact product gap remain decisive. Official pricing documentation states Forge app partner share and eligibility conditions; after 1-Oct-2026, the standard Forge partner share is 83% for eligible Forge apps above the first US$1M lifetime Forge revenue incentive. Actual terms must be checked at release time.

Sources:
- https://www.atlassian.com/blog/teamwork/atlassian-marketplace-7-billion
- https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/marketplace/pricing-payment-and-billing/

### 3.3 Shopify App Store — verified developer payouts and high-end examples
Shopify reported on 28-Apr-2026 that it paid developers more than **US$1.3B in 2025**, serves millions of merchants, and active app installs grew nearly 20% year-on-year. Shopify also profiled examples: Postscript serves 20,000+ brands and crossed US$100M revenue in the prior year; Locksmith had about 16,000 active subscribers and eight-figure revenue. These are selected success stories, not typical developer outcomes or an expected forecast.

Shopify's official revenue-share documentation says eligible developers retain 100% of the first US$1M lifetime gross app revenue, then 85% above that, subject to a 2.9% processing fee and a one-time US$19 App Store registration fee. The fee means the app-store route is not zero-spend by default and remains owner-gated.

Sources:
- https://www.shopify.com/news/billion-dollar-ecosystem
- https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share

### 3.4 Microsoft Marketplace — high-value B2B route, seller gates
Microsoft's current payout FAQ states a 3% store service fee (97% effective earnings rate for applicable offers), credit-card earnings mature after a 30-day hold, and payout/tax profiles must be validated. This can be attractive for high-value B2B software but is not frictionless: transactable offer requirements, hosting, seller verification and India-specific account eligibility must be proven.

Sources:

### 3.5 Apify Store — usage revenue, but margin and platform gate
Apify documents a 20% commission and deduction of infrastructure/proxy/storage usage costs from pay-per-event earnings; poor pricing can produce negative profit. Its help material reports top independent creators above US$10,000 MRR, but this is a platform-reported high-end example and not a typical outcome. Current P58 incident/post-mortem gate remains uncleared in the 14-Nov mission checkpoint.

### 3.6 Creator economy — large supply and growth, but repeat monetization is the bottleneck
The 2026 ISB SRITNE × Hashfame study reports 4.12M Indian creators in 2025, up from 0.96M in 2020, with 66% non-metro participation. Qoruz-based estimates show campaign volume rising from about 14,000 to 42,000 between 2020 and 2025, with average spend per campaign 3.6× higher. But non-metro campaign-to-creator ratio fell from about 0.37 to 0.10, meaning supply/activation breadth expanded faster than repeat monetization depth. This makes creator sponsorship/brand campaign income a weaker first core engine under the mission's no-cold-outreach/no-manual-fulfilment constraints; owned IP royalties and reusable content assets may still be separately tested as secondary streams.

Source: https://prodcd.isb.edu/media/ykmjlwaj/india_creator_economy_interactive_report.html

### 3.7 Books / digital IP — stackable secondary stream
Amazon KDP's official pricing page (effective 7-Jul-2026) lists a 70% royalty price band for India Kindle Store at ₹99–₹599, with territory and KDP Select eligibility conditions. This is a real self-serve royalty rail, but the low per-unit earning and need for discoverability/catalogue depth make it a portfolio supplement rather than the provisional largest first engine.

Source: https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560

### 3.8 Open-source sponsorship / stock assets
GitHub Sponsors says personal-account sponsorships carry no platform fee, while organization sponsorships can carry fees up to 6%. Envato's Author Fee structure states 50% for Envato Market. These are viable distribution/royalty channels but no existing audience, paid adoption or catalogue demand has been proven in this mission. Keep them in the portfolio universe; do not elevate them above B2B software on current evidence.

## 4. Provisional source-family order after evidence batch 01
This is a research priority, not a validated forecast:

1. **Productized B2B software / vertical SaaS / marketplace apps** — FIRST research lane. Best combined evidence for recurring business spend, high-value buyers, distribution and scalable repeat delivery.
2. **AI agents and APIs as software monetization layers** — evaluate as product capabilities or distinct API businesses; do not assume AI itself is a market or buyer.
3. **Software/IP licensing, OEM and white-label** — high-value potential, but validate repeatable self-serve/licensing terms and avoid recurring bespoke implementation.
4. **Digital products, technical publishing, books and reusable IP** — secondary stackable royalty/download stream; catalog and distribution evidence required.
5. **Creator sponsorship, affiliate and advertising** — keep as secondary until an owned audience or repeatable inbound distribution exists; creator-market participation alone is not income evidence.
6. **Stock assets, games and open-source sponsorship** — retain as portfolio options; require specific adoption/royalty evidence before production priority.

Marketplace rails are not a separate rank when they distribute the same product engine.

## 5. Largest-source hypothesis and required next research
**Working hypothesis:** build/monetize one differentiated B2B software product or a compact suite of interoperable vertical modules, using the highest-fit eligible marketplace/direct self-serve rail. The winning vertical is NOT yet selected.

Next compare:
- Existing owned/licensable software/IP assets versus greenfield build.
- ERP/workflow/finance/compliance/operations verticals with high-frequency pain and identifiable budget owners.
- Pricing capacity (per company, seat, location, transaction, workflow or usage) and realistic buyer counts.
- 10–15 direct/adjacent substitutes for each serious problem.
- Self-serve acquisition and onboarding without cold outreach or recurring manual implementation.
- India seller eligibility, payouts, legal entity, fees, hosting and support obligations.
- Conservative customer-count and price scenarios that show how a source can contribute materially to ₹10 Cr without claiming the whole target must come from it.

Do not launch multiple products simultaneously. First make the highest-ranked source's initial monetization loop work and become repeatable; then add the next source.
