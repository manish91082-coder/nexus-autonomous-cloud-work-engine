# Phase 20 — Solar Asset Manager and Recurring Reporting Workload Qualification
**Date:** 2026-11-12  
**Purpose:** Identify real operating portfolios and determine whether reporting QA can be purchased as a separable external work package.  
**Decision:** No qualified subcontract buyer or funded reporting assignment verified. Three evidence-backed target classes identified; one strong strategic channel candidate, two large integrated operators, and one regional low-scale operator.

## 1. Executive finding
Phase 20 moved from general O&M directories to companies with published portfolio, operating cadence, reporting scope or multi-site evidence.

The most relevant findings:
1. **SunKey Asset Management Services Pvt. Ltd.** explicitly markets multi-asset renewable asset management for owners, funds and lenders, including monthly operational reports, quarterly board packs and lender reporting. Its published scenario describes an investor with multiple solar/wind SPVs managed by different O&M vendors and inconsistent reports. This is an unusually close problem match. However, the multi-asset scenario is explicitly illustrative; it is not proof of a current customer, open overflow workload or willingness to subcontract. The firm itself offers the same service and has its own monitoring platform, so it may be a competitor/prime or partner rather than buyer.
2. **UVIE / Vibrant Solar** publicly states 31 renewable projects totalling 116.23 MWp as of 31-Aug-2026, with 24/7 monitoring, SCADA/RMS, meter-wise reconciliation and periodic reporting. This proves a meaningful operating workload, not independent subcontract demand. Its 25-member O&M engineering team and full-scope capabilities suggest reporting is internalized.
3. **KPI Green OMS** publishes 70 sites under management and 1,733 MW active O&M on one section of its site, and a 2.2 GW operational asset headline elsewhere. Its scope includes reporting, billing/collections support, compliance and audit documentation, supported by an NOC, IBM Maximo, SCADA and an in-house UDA AI agent. It appears highly integrated and technologically mature; weak near-term fit for an external reporting-only supplier.
4. **Luxsun Energy** publishes an identifiable Madhya Pradesh portfolio of 18 plants, 1.07 MW across six locations, including Satna, Rewa, Maihar, Khajuraho, Bhopal and Jhansi. It is geographically accessible and publishes plant-level O&M descriptions, but the total capacity is small and no buyer-side reporting outsource requirement is visible. It is a low-scale validation lead, not a qualified buyer.
5. **Inox Green** investor presentation describes a large multi-state renewable O&M portfolio (13.3 GWp total, including about 3.3 GWp solar) and 24/7 centralized monitoring; its Jan 2026 press release reports a 625 MWp solar O&M award from KEC. This is clear large-scale workload evidence, but the company is a major integrated operator with established NOC capabilities. Not a near-term reporting-only buyer absent a named overflow procurement.

## 4. What is now evidenced versus still unknown
**Evidenced by public company pages or releases:**
- recurring reporting obligations exist at operating solar portfolios;
- asset managers produce monthly operational reports and quarterly investor/board packs;
- SCADA/inverter/meter reconciliation and exception tracking are operational tasks;
- multi-OEM / multi-vendor portfolio standardisation is a recognized problem scenario;
- several operators maintain internal NOCs, teams or automated reporting platforms.

**Not evidenced:**
- a named company currently has unprocessed reporting backlog;
- reporting can legally be subcontracted independently under its customer contracts;
- any of these companies has approved an external vendor for this task;
- any budget, rate card, payment schedule, purchase order or funded work package;
- our company has the required production solar analytics track record;
- any company is willing to provide plant data to an unproven supplier.

## 5. Refined offer: reporting QA as a bounded back-office work package
Avoid selling a dashboard or a broad “AI solar platform”. Sell a measurable reduction in reporting preparation and reconciliation effort.

**Proposed scope:**
1. Receive authorized monthly CSV/XLSX exports and the buyer's data dictionary.
2. Validate timestamps, intervals, units, meter/site identifiers and duplicates.
3. Reconcile inverter, plant and billing-meter totals without silently overwriting source data.
4. Produce missing-data and discrepancy register with source references.
5. Calculate only buyer-approved metrics and formulas (generation, PR, CUF, availability, yield) with definitions supplied by the buyer.
6. Generate draft site and portfolio report in the buyer's existing template.
7. Produce reviewer checklist and immutable run log.
8. Handover with buyer sign-off; no independent engineering judgement.

**Pilot acceptance criteria:**
- every metric traces to source file and source row/interval;
- all gaps and mismatches explicitly reported;
- no interpolation or imputation without written rule;
- timezone, units, meter boundary and formula version declared;
- totals reproduce within agreed tolerance;
- reviewer can reproduce the report from the delivered inputs and instructions.

**Commercial structure to test, not yet validated:**
- one-plant, one-month historical data QA sample;
- fixed fee with 50% advance and 50% on accepted deliverable, or escrow/milestone;
- only scale to multi-site monthly retainer after time saved, exception closure and buyer acceptance are measured.
No price is asserted until sample volume, data quality, review time and buyer budget are known.

## 6. Go/no-go gates for any candidate
A candidate only becomes qualified when:
1. authorized decision-maker confirms a live operational reporting problem;
2. a named plant/portfolio and reporting period exist;
3. buyer confirms independent subcontracting is permitted;
4. written authorization covers data access and permitted processing;
5. redacted sample exports and current report template are provided securely;
6. data dictionary, timezone, meter hierarchy and formula definitions are available;
7. volume/cadence and named reviewer are defined;
8. objective acceptance criteria and change-control are agreed;
9. fee, taxes, payout route and fees are known;
10. advance/milestone/escrow protects payment before work starts;
11. security, confidentiality, IP, liability and prime-contract flow-down terms are approved;
12. expected net receipts remain positive after all actual costs.

## 8. Next phase
Phase 21 should investigate actual subcontractability and buying triggers through public evidence only:
- prime-contract subcontracting clauses in relevant O&M awards;
- vendor/partner registration pages that explicitly include analytics, data QA or reporting delivery;
- recent project awards followed by ramp-up, multi-site onboarding or reporting obligations;
- company procurement notices for MIS, data processing, SCADA integration support or analytics;
- public tenders that separately procure asset management, owner's engineer, monitoring or reporting services.
Do not infer an outsourcing need from a portfolio or job title. Do not contact any target without explicit user approval.
