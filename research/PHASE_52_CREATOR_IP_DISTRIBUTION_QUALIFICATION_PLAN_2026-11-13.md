## 2. Candidate platforms and existing assets

### A. GitHub Sponsors / developer ecosystem
Existing candidate: CSV to Action and other safe open-source assets in the canonical repository.

Questions:
- Are there active users, stars, forks, issues, repeat installs or community adoption?
- Does the product solve a recurring developer/business pain with an upgrade path, support or hosted service?
- Are relevant GitHub Marketplace listings paid, transactable or referral-only? What do competitor pricing and reviews show?
- Does sponsorship require a public open-source repo plus community goodwill rather than a repeatable product purchase?
- Can the asset be published as a self-serve tool without creating a second repository or violating owner IP/licence gates?

Hard gate: no new standalone public repository or MIT licensing without explicit owner confirmation of authorship and rights. Stars and downloads are not revenue.

### B. Amazon KDP / digital publishing
Candidate content must be original, rights-cleared and useful to a defined buyer.
Questions:
- Exact category, search competition, book pricing, ranking/review evidence, release cadence and royalty model.
- Whether there is a narrow underserved reader/buyer problem.
- Whether content can be produced from owned/reliable research without mass-generated low-quality content, copied material or fake reviews.
- Actual per-sale royalty after KDP print/ebook deductions and required order volume for ₹1Cr/₹10Cr.
- Payout timing and country availability from current official terms.

Hard gate: no bulk book generation or upload until category gap, original content standard, editorial quality, and economics are supported. Books are not a recurring service and are not automatically scalable from volume alone.

### C. Stock/design asset marketplaces
Potential IP: original diagrams, business templates, icons, video/motion assets, code components or other original media.
Questions:
- Which formats and categories show actual buyer transactions and recurring licensing activity?
- What are current contributor acceptance rules, exclusivity, commission/royalty, payout thresholds, review time and regional eligibility?
- How crowded are the top categories and how much catalog depth is required to earn?
- Can assets be genuinely differentiated and produced at scale without copyright/trademark misuse or generic AI slop?
- Are per-asset economics positive after platform share and time/compute costs?

Hard gate: no mass-upload catalogue without evidence of licensing demand, clear IP rights and sustainable quality.

### D. Creator audience / affiliate channels
Questions:
- Is there a real existing audience owned by the user or a platform-native discovery mechanism?
- Which products have an affiliate programme with published commissions, attribution window, payout threshold and India eligibility?
- Can acquisition be self-serve/content-led without cold outreach, spam, paid traffic or recurring manual sales?
- What traffic and conversion volume would mathematically be needed for the target?
- Does the model compound through evergreen content or repeat buyer usage?

Hard gate: do not count social followers, views, clicks, likes, referrals or an affiliate link as revenue. Count only valid credited commissions after reversals.

## 4. Required evidence collection

P52-001 must:
1. Inspect each platform's official current seller/contributor/author rules, current rates/fees, country eligibility, payout thresholds and review/acceptance process.
2. Audit at least 10–15 directly relevant competitors per shortlisted platform where public evidence is available; capture price, ratings/reviews, update cadence, product quality, licensing, visible adoption and any published transaction/rank signals.
3. Map at least three candidate buyer problems, then identify existing assets that solve each problem.
4. Separate verified platform facts, platform/vendor claims, third-party analytics, anecdotal community evidence and analyst hypotheses.
5. Model orders/licences/downloads/sponsorships required for ₹1L, ₹1Cr and ₹10Cr PCNE after platform fees, reversals and directly attributable costs.
6. Explicitly identify whether distribution is organic/platform-native, creator-audience-dependent, paid-acquisition-dependent or manual-sales-dependent.
7. Check whether each candidate satisfies the no-employment/no-freelance/no-cold-outreach/no-recurring-manual-labour constraint.
8. Rank all surviving opportunities by risk-adjusted scalable net revenue, not by gross market size or vanity metrics.
9. Make a GO/NO-GO decision for one candidate only. If none qualifies, NO-GO all and broaden the platform search using current primary sources.

## 5. Scoring framework

Use 100 points with documented evidence and a confidence level:
- Proven buyer spending / recurring demand: 25
- Risk-adjusted revenue scale and unit economics: 20
- Platform-native distribution / customer acquisition: 15
- Self-serve delivery and automation: 15
- Defensible IP/product gap: 10
- Zero-upfront feasibility / runtime costs: 10
- Rights, policy and operational risk: 5

Apply hard vetoes regardless of score:
- No proven legal/right-to-use basis.
- Requires cold outreach, freelance labour, employment or recurring service work as the primary model.
- No plausible customer discovery route.
- Negative or unbounded variable cost.
- Platform prohibits the category or country/seller path is not eligible.
- Needs unapproved spending, account actions, public release or owner-only declarations.

A high score without independent buyer evidence cannot be called GO.
