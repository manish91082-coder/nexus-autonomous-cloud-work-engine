# Orbit Relay — Human Playtest Protocol v1.0

**Status:** Prepared; not yet executed. This is a qualitative pilot, not a statistically representative market study.

## Test design
- Recruit 5–8 independent testers, mixing casual puzzle players and people who rarely play puzzle games.
- Use the same private build. No accounts, installs, personal data or payments.
- Observe silently. If blocked for more than 60 seconds, intervene and record it.
- Session target: 10–15 minutes. Obtain consent before any screen/audio recording; observer notes are preferred.

## Neutral session script
1. Say: “Please explore this game and tell me what you think as you play. I will not guide you unless you become completely stuck.”
2. Start timer when start screen appears.
3. Observe whether Campaign and Daily Challenge are identified without help.
4. Ask tester to begin Campaign. Do not point to controls.
5. Record time to first meaningful action, hesitation, incorrect taps, menu exits and help requests.
6. Allow up to 5 minutes of Campaign play; record levels completed, hints, retries and confusion.
7. Ask tester to return to menu and find Daily Challenge; record whether the distinction is understood.
8. Allow up to 3 minutes of Daily Challenge play.
9. Offer: “Would you like to play another round, stop here, or explore something else?” Record actual choice and replay.
10. Ask: What was confusing? When were you most engaged/frustrated? What made you stop? What would you change first? Would you recommend it to someone who enjoys this type of puzzle, and why?

Avoid leading questions such as “Was it fun?” or premature “Would you pay?” questions. Observed voluntary replay is stronger evidence than stated enthusiasm.

## Anonymous observation record
For each session ID T01–T08 capture device/viewport/browser/input method; time to first action; time to first completion or not completed; Campaign levels attempted/completed; Daily Challenge discovered/started/completed; hints/retries/accidental actions/menu exits; help interventions; voluntary replay choice and actual replay; top three friction points in anonymized words; outcome (independent / with help / abandoned).

## Directional thresholds
With 5–8 testers this is defect discovery, not statistical market validation.
- Critical usability issue: 2+ testers independently fail the same core task. Fix before distribution.
- Navigation: target at least 6/8 identify and start a mode without guidance.
- Core loop: target at least 6/8 make a meaningful move within 30 seconds.
- Voluntary replay: fewer than 3/8 actual replays means revisit the loop before adding content.
- Any reproducible crash, data loss, blocked interaction or unusable viewport is a release blocker.

Passing these thresholds does not prove product-market fit, retention, platform acceptance or revenue. A larger cohort and real distribution data are still required.

## Evidence ladder
1. Usability: tester can understand and operate the game.
2. Engagement: tester voluntarily replays without prompting.
3. Retention: tester returns another day without operator reminder.
4. Distribution: independent users arrive from a real channel.
5. Monetization: platform reports eligible revenue and payout settles.

Do not skip a rung. Compliments, hypothetical payment intent, test plays, downloads and views are not realized receipts.

## Privacy and integrity
Use anonymous IDs; do not collect player contact details. Do not upload recordings without explicit consent. Do not fabricate or infer outcomes. Record date, build hash and cohort size. Keep raw notes separate from public marketing claims.

## Stop / go
- STOP: reproducible crash, severe confusion, privacy/rights concern or platform-policy uncertainty.
- ITERATE: repeated usability friction or low voluntary replay.
- CONTINUE VALIDATION: no critical defects and encouraging voluntary replay, but no market-fit claim.
- PUBLIC RELEASE: only after title/IP review, platform account/terms, KYC/tax/payout setup and explicit owner approval.
