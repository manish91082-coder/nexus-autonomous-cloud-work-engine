## 1. Decision rule
Do not optimize for the fastest small sale alone, nor for the largest theoretical TAM. Identify the largest **credible risk-adjusted cumulative net-revenue source** that can be reached under the user's constraints, then execute it first. Add additional sources after the first source has a verified repeatable monetization loop; continue aggregating their PCNE while earlier sources remain active.

## 2. Required opportunity families to compare
At minimum, evaluate:
1. Recurring B2B SaaS / vertical software.
2. Marketplace apps and extensions (Atlassian, Shopify, Microsoft, Google Workspace, Zoho, monday, Salesforce, WordPress and other eligible ecosystems).
3. AI agents, APIs and usage-based software.
4. Self-serve digital products, templates, datasets and downloadable utilities.
5. Software/IP licensing, white-label and OEM licensing where self-serve or low-touch.
6. Creator IP, books, technical publishing, courses and royalties.
7. Stock assets and reusable media libraries.
8. Games and software distribution.
9. Sponsorship / open-source support.
10. Affiliate and advertising revenue only where a compliant owned distribution asset/audience exists.
11. Other lawful, zero-upfront or owner-approved, highly automated Internet-native models discovered during research.

These are source families, not automatic product approvals. Exclude employment, freelance bidding, manual gigs, cold outreach, spam, fake engagement, unauthorized data use, prohibited platform activity and recurring manual fulfilment.

## 3. Comparative scorecard (100 points)
| Dimension | Weight | Evidence required |
|---|---:|---|
| Credible cumulative net-revenue ceiling | 20 | Paying market, price, reachable buyer count, conservative obtainable share |
| Buyer/payment evidence | 15 | Real purchases, paid competitor use, buyer problem and budget signals |
| Repeatability / recurring potential | 15 | Renewal, repeat purchase, usage, licensing or catalogue compounding |
| Distribution / acquisition | 12 | Marketplace search, organic discovery, existing channel or self-serve loop |
| Time to first verified earnings | 8 | Concrete path, eligibility and onboarding requirements |
| Automation / marginal delivery cost | 10 | Setup once, repeatable fulfilment, support and failure handling |
| Net unit economics / capital intensity | 10 | Fees, runtime, refunds, payment costs, support and zero-spend feasibility |
| Rights / platform / payout / operational feasibility | 10 | Official terms, source rights, India seller eligibility, risk and owner gates |

A high score does not override a hard legal, rights, safety, seller-eligibility or unit-economics failure. Scores must include evidence confidence and unresolved unknowns; unknowns are not silently scored as positive.

## 4. Required output per source
- Source family and specific business model.
- Buyer, job-to-be-done, trigger, price and buying route.
- TAM/SAM/SOM only where sourced; distinguish market size from obtainable revenue.
- Conservative/base/upside scenarios, with assumptions and sensitivity.
- Expected contribution margin after every attributable cost.
- Route to first paid transaction and platform/provider credited earnings.
- Scale mechanics, retention, distribution, concentration and failure risks.
- Legal/data rights, account eligibility, payout and owner gates.
- Candidate NO-GO versus parent-source status (OPEN/HOLD/CLOSED).
- Recommendation: FIRST / NEXT / HOLD / NO-GO with confidence.

## 5. Portfolio sequencing invariant
First complete the highest-ranked source's first repeatable monetization engine: independent paid transaction, PCNE reconciliation, self-serve delivery, positive fully-loaded unit economics and a measured repeatability path. "Complete" does not mean waiting for that source to reach ₹10 Cr. Then activate the next highest-ranked source while the first continues earning. Only one new product build is active by default; multiple source families may be researched in parallel and multiple validated streams may earn concurrently.
